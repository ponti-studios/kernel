# Hiring

_How we evaluate people. Companion to [[principles|Operating Principles]]._

## Decision rule

Hire when a candidate clears the capability bar, improves the studio's perspective, and has a working style the role can sustain. A strong interview is evidence collection, not a performance contest.

## Before opening a role

Write a one-page scorecard before speaking to candidates:

- **Outcome:** what must be true six months after the hire?
- **Capabilities:** which skills are required on day one?
- **Judgment:** which decisions will this person make without supervision?
- **Perspective:** what does the current team fail to see or do well?
- **Constraints:** what is genuinely non-negotiable, and what can be learned?

If the team cannot agree on the scorecard, it is not ready to interview.

## What to evaluate

### Capability

Look for evidence that the candidate has done comparable work, understands tradeoffs, and can maintain what they build. Greenfield excitement is easy; caring about what remains afterward is the differentiator.

### Judgment

Ask what they would clarify before acting, what they would do with incomplete information, and how they would decide when the pragmatic solution is better than the perfect one.

### Self-awareness

Ask what they are looking for, what they want to learn, what they are doing now, and where they want to be in a year. The point is not ambition theater; it is to test whether the role can honestly support the person.

### Translation

At Ponti Studios, people move between founders, designers, engineers, and end users. Test whether the candidate can preserve the idea while changing the language.

### Perspective

Record the specific assumption, background, or way of seeing that the candidate adds. "Culture fit" is not a substitute for this analysis.

## Interview loop

1. **Context:** explain the problem space, product, role, and constraints.
2. **Motivation:** ask what the candidate wants and what they want to learn.
3. **Evidence:** examine comparable work, including a failure or maintenance decision.
4. **Working session:** present an ambiguous problem and observe questions, tradeoffs, and communication.
5. **Translation:** ask the candidate to explain the same decision to two different audiences.
6. **Candidate questions:** treat the questions as evidence of priorities and preparation.

Do not use trick questions, optimize for a memorized answer, or treat technical recall as a proxy for capability.

## Decision record

Every interviewer submits evidence independently before discussion:

- What requirement did the evidence support?
- What requirement did it fail to support?
- What remains unknown?
- What perspective would this person add?
- What is the highest-risk assumption behind a yes?

The final decision should name the capability gap filled and the perspective added. If the rationale is only that everyone liked the candidate, the process failed.

## Review after hiring

At 30, 90, and 180 days, compare the scorecard with reality. Retire requirements that were ornamental, add signals that were missing, and record whether the original perspective actually changed decisions.
