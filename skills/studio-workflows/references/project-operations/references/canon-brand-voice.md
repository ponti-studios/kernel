# Brand Voice

_How we speak._

This document is the verbal constitution of Ponti Studios. It translates our philosophical principles into writing craft — how we name, describe, headline, and close every piece of copy we produce. It is both reference and doctrine: the standard against which we measure our own words.

Where [[film-philosophy]] governs the frame, this document governs the sentence. They are the same identity expressed through different senses.

For the mechanics of writing — naming rules, headline structure, body copy standards, CTAs, and context-specific guidance — see [[brand-copy]]. For the editing hierarchy, checklist, and banned words, see [[brand-editing]].

## Verbal Philosophy

Our four principles — Kanso, Ma, Shibui, Wabi-sabi — are not visual abstractions. They are writing disciplines applied to every sentence.

### Kanso (簡素) — The Sentence as Essential Statement

**Declaration:** Every word must earn its place. If it does not serve clarity or conviction, it is noise.

**In practice:**

- We do not decorate sentences; we build them. An adjective is not a flourish — it is either evidence or it is removed.
- When we describe what we do, we name the outcome, not the mechanism. "We ship products that work" beats "We leverage cutting-edge technology to drive transformative outcomes."
- Product names should use the shortest name that carries the concept. Any name that requires explaining is wrong.
- Strip the sentence to its functional minimum. Then evaluate whether to add.

**Reference:** Hemingway's iceberg principle — the sentence is the tip; everything the reader needs to know is visible, everything that supports it is submerged. We apply the same rigor: the page is not written, it is **edited down to truth**.

### Ma (間) — The Power of the Pause in Prose

**Declaration:** White space — visual pauses between sentences, sections, and ideas — is not empty. It is structural.

**In practice:**

- In copy, we do not fear a one-sentence paragraph. A line standing alone is not incomplete — it is the reader catching up to weight.
- In structure, we let the page breathe. Not every gap needs filling. We leave room for the reader to sit with an idea before the next one arrives.
- In pacing, we alternate between density and release. A tight paragraph of claims earns its power only when preceded or followed by air.
- The pause is where the reader decides. Do not rob them of it.

**Reference:** The best product pages use generous leading, short lines, and deliberate section breaks. The space between elements is a compositional tool, not a layout default. The reader's eye should rest, and in that rest, resonance accumulates.

### Shibui (渋い) — Restraint as Authority

**Declaration:** We reject verbal inflation. Our copy does not shout to hold attention — it earns it through precision. Understatement is our default. Emphasis is our exception.

**In practice:**

- A flat declarative sentence is not a weakness — it is a declaration of confidence. "We build tools that work" does not need an exclamation point.
- When we emphasize, we emphasize with structure, not volume. A sentence set alone. A colon that demands the next line carry its weight. Bold is earned, never default.
- We do not use superlatives as a stylistic crutch. "Best," "greatest," "revolutionary" — these are not claims, they are placeholders for missing evidence.
- Periods are our punctuation of choice. Exclamation points are reserved for moments that genuinely warrant excitement — not for making flat copy feel urgent.

**Reference:** Apple's product copy. "Light. Years ahead." Seven characters. Two sentences. No verbs. The restraint is the point. We adopt the same discipline: if you cannot state it plainly, you do not understand it well enough to state it at all.

### Wabi-sabi (侘寂) — Honest Language

**Declaration:** We do not hide the truth of our products behind polished copy. The grain of an honest limitation is stronger than a perfect claim.

**In practice:**

- We prefer direct description over marketing gloss. "In early access" beats "Revolutionizing the future of X."
- We do not overpromise in headlines what the body cannot support. If the product has rough edges, the copy does not pretend otherwise — it frames them honestly.
- When we use plain language (as in our Monotone writing), we do not apologize for it. Directness is not a failure of craft — it is a choice that respects the reader's time.
- Editing is applied to serve clarity, not to inflate authority. If a word adds no information, it is cut.

**Reference:** Stripe's documentation voice — technical, precise, never condescending. The prose does not sell the product; it explains what the product does, clearly enough that the reader can decide for themselves. We apply the same standard: the reader is intelligent and busy. Write for them.

## Voice

### Our Voice

Ponti Studios speaks in the first person. "I" and "we" and "you." We do not hide behind passive constructions or corporate plurals.

**Tenets of voice:**

1. **Direct address.** We talk to the reader as one person to another. No "users," no "customers," no "the end user." "You" and "your."

2. **Declarative, not defensive.** We state what we do and what we believe. We do not hedge ("we believe," "we think," "we aim to") unless the claim genuinely requires qualification.

3. **Specific, not abstract.** We name actual things. Actual products. Actual numbers. Actual outcomes. "I reduced hosting costs by 40%" beats "I drive operational efficiency."

4. **Calm authority.** We do not beg for attention. We do not use urgency as a rhetorical device. The work speaks loud enough.

### What We Sound Like

| Instead of | We write |
|---|---|
| "Leverage our platform to drive transformative business outcomes" | "This tool saves you time. Here's how." |
| "We're revolutionizing the way people think about personal productivity" | "A notes app that doesn't get in your way." |
| "Our mission is to empower creators through innovative AI solutions" | "We build tools that make your ideas easier to capture." |
| "Utilizing state-of-the-art machine learning algorithms" | "It learns what matters to you." |

### What We Do Not Sound Like

- **Startup jargon.** "Synergy," "paradigm shift," "growth hacking," "deep dive," "circle back." These words are not permitted in external copy.
- **Corporate passive.** "It has been determined that..." — no. Say who determined what.
- **Overfamiliar hustle.** "Hey fam," "crushing it," "fire," "let's goooo." We are not a DTC brand on Twitter.
- **Defensive positioning.** "Unlike our competitors who..." — we do not name competitors in copy. Our work is the differentiator.
