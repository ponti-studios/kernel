# Film Sound

_The audience hears what the character feels._

This document covers the sonic craft of Ponti Studios film work: how we exaggerate sound for story, use silence as structure, and deploy music with restraint. It assumes the philosophy established in [[film-philosophy]].

For the visual edit, see [[film-editing]]. For on-set craft, see [[film-capture]].

---

## Exaggeration for Story

Fincher: exaggerate sounds that are meant to tell the story. Align sound with the character's experience.

**Our principle:** Sound is not a naturalistic record of the world. It is an amplification of the character's perception.

- The rustle of a jacket in a tense moment is louder than it would be in reality — because the character hears it.
- The hum of a refrigerator in an empty room is a character in itself.
- We build soundscapes from the character's emotional state, not from the physical reality of the location. A happy scene sounds open and warm. A paranoid scene sounds close and textured.

---

## Silence as Structure (Ma in Sound)

- Silence before a sound makes the sound matter.
- We strip ambience when the scene requires focus. The absence of room tone is a magnifying glass on the dialogue or the action.
- We do not fill silence with music. Silence is not a problem to be solved — it is a resource to be deployed.

---

## Music

Music is not a mood setter. It is the last resort — the tool we reach for when the image and sound design are not enough.

- **Diegetic music first.** Music that exists in the world of the scene (a radio, a band, a character's headphones) is always stronger than a score.
- **Score as character.** When we do score, the music belongs to the protagonist's emotional state, not the scene's objective tone. The audience should feel _with_ the character, not _about_ the scene.
- **Rhythm.** The edit does not follow the music. The music follows the edit, or they exist independently. Cutting to the beat is a music video technique, not a cinematic one.
- **Silence is scoring.** A scene with no music is not lacking — it is trusting the audience to feel without being told how.
