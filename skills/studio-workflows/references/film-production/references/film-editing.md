# Film Editing

_The cut is where the meaning lives._

This document covers the editorial craft of Ponti Studios film work: how we cut subjectively, build rhythm through negative space, and use punctuation. It assumes the philosophy established in [[film-philosophy]].

For on-set craft, see [[film-capture]]. For audio, see [[film-sound]].

---

## Subjective Cutting

Fincher: if the character notices something, we see what they see. The edit follows the character's train of thought, not an omniscient observer's.

**Our rule:** Every cut must answer: who is experiencing this moment? The edit belongs to that character. We see what they see, we hear what they hear, we feel time as they feel it.

This means:

- Cut to what the character looks at before the audience wonders what they are looking at.
- Hold on a character's reaction before cutting to the subject of their gaze.
- Use insert shots not for information but for subjective emphasis — the character notices the ring, the crack in the glass, the sweat on someone's brow.

---

## Rhythm and Negative Space (Ma in Editing)

- A sequence of rapid cuts accumulates energy. We let it run until the audience needs relief, then we give them a held beat. The release is where the emotion registers.
- We do not cut on every line of dialogue. We hold on a listening face, let the silence after a sentence carry weight.
- The J-cut and L-cut are tools, not defaults. We use them when the overlap of sound and image serves the narrative — not because smooth transitions are inherently better.
- A hard cut is honest. Dissolves and fades are decisions, not apologies. We do not use a dissolve to soften a cut we lack the courage to commit to.

---

## Punctuation

Anderson uses whip pans as punctuation. Fincher uses hard cuts as punctuation. We use both, deliberately:

- **Whip pan:** transition between two moments that are tonally or spatially adjacent but formally distinct. The whip says: we are moving to a new part of the same world.
- **Hard cut:** the default. Two moments placed next to each other without apology. The cut says: this follows this, and the meaning is in their collision.
- **Dissolve:** reserved for shifts in time, memory, or consciousness. A dissolve is an intrusion of the past into the present, or a dream into reality. We do not dissolve for the sake of transition.
- **Fade to black:** an ending, a loss, a reset. Used sparingly, it carries the weight of death or rebirth.
