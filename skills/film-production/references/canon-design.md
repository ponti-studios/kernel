# Design

_Operational version of [[principles|Operating Principles]] and [[film-production-design|Film Production Design]]. Design should make the useful clear, give it room to breathe, and make its logic transferable._

## Decision rule

Design for the person's real decision or action. Every element must either clarify the problem, enable the action, establish hierarchy, or carry meaningful character. If it does none of these, remove it.

## Start with the situation

Before choosing a layout or style, write:

- Who is here?
- What are they trying to do?
- What do they already know?
- What decision or action must become easier?
- What uncertainty or friction currently blocks them?

The artifact should answer these questions through its behavior and form, not through a paragraph explaining the design.

## Design sequence

1. **Frame the outcome:** define what success looks like for the person.
2. **Reduce the path:** remove steps, choices, and information that do not serve the outcome.
3. **Establish hierarchy:** make the primary action and primary idea unmistakable.
4. **Add evidence:** use content, states, examples, and feedback to make the system legible.
5. **Give it character:** use color, type, spacing, motion, environment, wardrobe, or props to express identity after function is sound.
6. **Test the handoff:** verify that another person can understand, extend, and operate what was designed.

## Core principles

### Function over decoration

Beauty is the result of attention: a clear idea, a considered form, and a system that behaves as it should. Do not add visual interest to disguise an unresolved product problem.

### Space is structure

Whitespace, silence, and pauses are active compositional tools. Use density for connected information and release for decisions that need room. Do not fill space because it is available.

### Character through evidence

In a product, the character comes from consistent choices and behavior. In film, the environment makes psychology physical: locations, wardrobe, and props should reveal something rather than decorate the frame.

### Transferability

The design system, rationale, and constraints should be understandable by the next person. Name patterns, preserve decisions, and avoid one-off cleverness that cannot be maintained.

## Interface gate

- Is the primary user and outcome explicit?
- Can the hierarchy be understood at a glance?
- Does every control describe or produce a meaningful outcome?
- Are empty, loading, error, success, and edge states designed?
- Does the interface leave enough room for comprehension?
- Does the visual language reinforce rather than compete with the task?
- Can another person extend the system without guessing?

## Environment and frame gate

- Does the environment reveal character, context, or stakes?
- Does every object in frame serve the narrative or get removed?
- Is the composition chosen for the intended relationship between person and world?
- Is graphic, planar composition being used because the scene needs clarity or construction—not because it is fashionable?
- Do wardrobe and props communicate profession, class, psychology, or transformation?

## Review loop

Review the work in this order: outcome, comprehension, hierarchy, behavior, system coherence, then finish. If the work is beautiful but the next action is unclear, it is not finished.
