# Brand Copy

_The mechanics._

This document covers the practical standards for Ponti Studios copy: how we name things, structure headlines, write body copy, design calls to action, and adapt for different contexts.

For the philosophy and voice that underpin these rules, see [[brand-voice]]. For the editing hierarchy and quality gate, see [[brand-editing]].

---

## Copy Principles

### Naming

- A name is a container. It should hold the concept without leaking.
- One word is better than two. Two is better than three. If the name needs a hyphen or a compound, keep looking.
- Names are not descriptors. "Hominem" is better than "PersonalAI." A name is a flag planted in the mind; a descriptor is coordinates on a map.
- Before naming, ask: does this improve clarity, desirability, memorability, or strategic coherence? If not, the name has not earned its place.

### Headlines

- The headline must survive alone. If a reader sees only the headline, they should know what the thing is and why it matters.
- No question headlines in external copy. "What if you could..." is a crutch. Declare.
- No puns as primary headlines. Puns are dessert, not the main course.
- A headline should fit on one line at the target breakpoint. If it wraps awkwardly, rewrite it.

### Body Copy

- One idea per paragraph. If a paragraph contains two ideas, split it.
- The first sentence of any section is the thesis. The sentences that follow are support. If the first sentence cannot stand as a summary, the section is not structured.
- Short sentences. Varied length within that constraint. A string of equal-length sentences reads like a drum machine.
- Specific beats generic. "I worked on search at StreamYard" beats "I have extensive product experience."
- Delete every word that can be deleted without changing the meaning. Then delete one more.

### Calls to Action

- CTAs state what happens next, not what the company hopes will happen. "Book a call" is a CTA. "Let's connect" is a wish.
- No imperative stacked on imperative. One action per CTA.
- The CTA should survive a scan. If it needs the surrounding paragraph to make sense, it is not a CTA.

### Descriptions

A product description follows a pattern:

1. **What it does** — one sentence. No tech. No qualifiers.
2. **Who it is for** — one sentence. Specific enough that the reader knows if they are included or excluded.
3. **How it works** — three sentences max. The mechanism, not the architecture.
4. **What it costs** — clear. If it is free, say "Free." If it has a price, state it. If it is not yet available, say "In development."

(Ponti Studios does not have pricing pages that require a sales call to reveal the number.)

## Writing for Context

### Web Copy

- The first paragraph must work as a meta description. Assume nothing above the fold survives a skim.
- Links are verbs. "Read the case study" not "click here."
- Bullet lists for comparison, enumeration, and options. Prose for argument and narrative.
- Every page is a landing page. Write as though the reader arrived without context.

### Product Copy

- Error messages are blame-free. "Something went wrong" beats "You entered an invalid value."
- Empty states are invitations. "No tasks yet" is honest. "Add your first task" is the next step.
- Buttons describe the outcome. "Save and continue" not "Submit."

### Case Studies / Project Pages

- Problem → approach → outcome. Always in that order.
- The outcome is stated first in the summary. The reader should know the result before they know the method.
- Numbers anchor the narrative. "Reduced API costs by 90%" is the headline; the story explains how.
- No testimonials without context. "John loved working with us" is noise. "John's team reduced deployment time from 4 weeks to 3 days" is evidence.

### Social & Short-form

- Lead with the thing, not the announcement. "I wrote about X" is worse than the actual insight.
- Threads are structure, not volume. Every tweet should survive alone.
- No call to action that asks for engagement without offering value. "RT if you agree" is out. "Here's what I learned about X — a thread" is acceptable if the thread justifies it.
