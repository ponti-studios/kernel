# Marketing

**Ponti Studios markets its products by demonstrating value, not by manufacturing attention.**

Use [[voice]] for copy quality and [[design]] for the evidence, hierarchy, and visual form of the thing being promoted.

Marketing is part of the product promise. The audience should leave with a clearer understanding of the problem, the product's actual usefulness, and the next sensible action.

## The marketing brief

Before producing a campaign or significant post, write:

- **Audience:** who has this problem now?
- **Problem:** what are they trying to do, and what gets in the way?
- **Evidence:** what can we show rather than merely claim?
- **Promise:** what will be different for the person who uses the product?
- **Limit:** who is this not for, or what does the product not do yet?
- **Action:** what is the smallest useful next step?

If the brief cannot answer these questions, the work is not ready to distribute.

## Non-negotiables

### Value before attention

Lead with the user's problem, not the feature list. Use plain language, screenshots, demos, and real use cases. Hype, vague claims, and manufactured urgency are off-brand.

### Honest positioning

Do not overstate capabilities, hide limitations, or make comparisons that depend on cherry-picked conditions. Say when a product is early, incomplete, or experimental.

### Relevant distribution

Share where the audience genuinely gathers. Participate in communities as a participant, not as a promotional account wearing an organic costume. If amplifying someone else's work, add context.

### No attention arbitrage

Ponti Studios will not use advertising as a revenue model or run paid campaigns that exploit attention mechanics. Time spent is not the same as value delivered.

## Channel test

Treat a new channel as an experiment, not a commitment:

1. State the audience and hypothesis.
2. Publish a small number of useful examples.
3. Measure qualified responses, not impressions alone.
4. Read replies and conversations for evidence of understanding.
5. Continue, change, or stop based on whether the channel creates useful product discovery.

## Approval gates

Project-owner sign-off is required for press releases, media pitches, paid promotions, partnerships, sponsorships, and campaigns naming a competitor. Do not accept paid placement or sponsored content without explicit approval.

## Post-publish review

Record what was published, where, for whom, and what happened. Review whether people understood the promise, whether the audience was relevant, and whether the resulting traffic showed intent. A campaign that gets attention but creates confusion is a failure.

## Pre-publish checklist

- Is the audience and problem specific?
- Is there evidence behind the promise?
- Are limitations and maturity visible?
- Is the channel relevant rather than merely large?
- Is the next action clear and proportionate?
- Has every required approval been recorded?
