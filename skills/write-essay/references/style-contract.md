# Style Contract

A quick-reference checklist for editors. This is what the voice does, this is what it never does, with examples.

---

## The Deal

Every essay makes exactly one claim. It builds that claim around a single metaphor. It is 300–800 words. It has deliberately uneven paragraph pacing. It does not explain itself. It ends on a strike.

---

## What It Does

### Opens cold
No context. No background. No "In recent years..." The first sentence is already in the argument.

> ✓ You think you are talking to intelligence. You are not.

> ✓ The most reliable lie a person tells themselves is that their intentions count. They don't.

> ✗ In recent years, artificial intelligence has become a topic of intense debate.

### Builds around one metaphor
The metaphor IS the argument. It's introduced by paragraph 2 and carries the whole piece.

> ✓ This is the Stradivarius problem. A perfect violin makes zero sound until a musician picks it up and plays.

> ✓ The mind is not a warehouse waiting to be filled. It is an orchestra.

### Uses direct address
Talks TO the reader. Not about them.

> ✓ You think you're watching intelligence. You're watching a text-prediction machine.

> ✗ One might observe that what appears to be intelligence is, in fact, text prediction.

### Varies paragraph length deliberately
A one-sentence paragraph. Then four. Then one. Then six. The eye never settles.

> ✓ It isn't.
>
> The entire debate about whether AI is "really intelligent" is a distraction. Humans don't even understand how we ourselves think. We are flawed improvisational creatures. Demanding that machines replicate that is a weird goal.
>
> That is the problem.

### Ends on a strike
Not a summary. Not a call to action. A sentence that lands and stops.

> ✓ The instrument is built. We need artists to play it.

> ✓ The quiet hum of a well-tuned instrument beats louder than any stored memory.

> ✗ In conclusion, the evidence suggests that...

### Uses fragments
A fragment that completes a thought is better than a full sentence that adds nothing.

> ✓ Not because commands are cool. Because commands imply intent plus action.

---

## What It Never Does

### Opens with context
> ✗ The field of artificial intelligence has seen remarkable progress in recent years...

### Uses academic transitions
> ✗ Thus, it follows that... / Therefore, we must consider... / However, it is important to note... / Furthermore, the evidence suggests...

### Uses "we" to create false consensus
> ✗ We often find ourselves... / We tend to believe that... / As a society, we...

### Explains the metaphor
> ✗ In other words, what I mean by the Stradivarius problem is... (The reader got it. Trust them.)

### Uses italics or bold for emphasis
> ✗ This is *really* important. / The **key** insight is...

If the sentence needs typography to land, the sentence isn't strong enough.

### Ends with a question
> ✗ So what will you do about it? (Questions open. Strikes close.)

### Cites statistics or studies
> ✗ According to a 2023 McKinsey report... (This is an argument from observation, not a report.)

### Uses hedge words
> ✗ Arguably / Interestingly / Notably / Perhaps / Some might say / It could be argued

### Uses exclamation points or emoji
> ✗ This changes everything! / The future is here 🚀

### Has a "key takeaways" section
The essay IS the takeaway. If you need to list them, the essay didn't do its job.

### Has uniform paragraph lengths
If every paragraph is 3-4 sentences, it reads like AI. The rhythm must surprise.

---

## The Litmus Test

Read the essay aloud silently. Ask:

1. Does the first sentence make me pause or argue?
2. Can I see the metaphor? Does it carry the whole piece?
3. Are paragraph lengths visibly different from each other?
4. Did I use "thus," "therefore," "however," "furthermore" anywhere?
5. Does the last line land — or does it explain?
6. Would I know this wasn't written by ChatGPT?
