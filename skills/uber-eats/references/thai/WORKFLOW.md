# Thai

Thai menus are built around a curry, a noodle, a rice and a vegetable — which maps neatly onto two people. Fish sauce and shrimp paste are fine here; the meat is the only thing to avoid.

## Basket for two, £35–45

- 1 curry
- 1 noodle dish (pad thai or similar)
- 1 vegetable side or stir-fry
- 1 rice, or sticky rice if the curry is Isaan-style
- 1 starter to share
- drinks if a deal includes them

## Order this

- **Curry:** green, red, massaman, panang, jungle — vegetable, tofu or prawn
- **Noodles:** pad thai (veg or prawn), pad see ew, drunken noodles with tofu
- **Vegetables:** morning glory, pak boong, garlic broccoli, stir-fried aubergine
- **Starters:** spring rolls, vegetable tempura, corn cakes, tofu satay
- **Rice:** jasmine, sticky rice, coconut rice
- **Sides:** som tam (papaya salad), cucumber relish

## Grep the menu for these words and skip them

`chicken`, `beef`, `pork`, `larb`, `nam tok`, `crying tiger`, `khao soi` (usually chicken), `satay` (unless tofu), `massaman` unless it names a vegetable, `duck`.

## Traps

- **Curries default to chicken.** The menu gives one description and the kitchen uses the same sauce for everything — order "vegetable green curry" or "prawn green curry" in words, not just by dish name.
- **"Pad thai" is not automatically vegetarian**, but it is fine here: tamarind, fish sauce and dried shrimp are all allowed. Do check it isn't listed with chicken.
- **"Pad krapow" means holy basil stir-fry**, and it is mince by default. Ask for tofu.
- **Thai salads can be meat-led.** Som tam is safe; larb and nam tok are not.
- **Spice level is a real question** at Thai places — if the user hasn't said, choose medium rather than "Thai hot".

## Rough prices

Curries £10–13 · noodles £10–12 · vegetable side £6–8 · rice £3–4 · starter £6–7. One of each lands £38–42 before fees, so pick the curry and noodle carefully rather than adding a third main.
