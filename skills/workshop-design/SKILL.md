# Workshop Design

Design workshops that produce decisions and artifacts, not discussion. A
workshop earns the room only if it must end with a decision made or an
artifact built — if it needs neither, it is a meeting, and this skill refuses
the job. The method below is synthesized from the GV Design Sprint
methodology (map, sketch, decide, prototype, test) and standard facilitation
practice: diverge before converging, timebox everything, and put the
decision-maker in the room.

## Method

### 1. Frame the workshop

Extract these from the request; ask when they are missing and stop there,
because format and agenda follow from them:

- **Outcome** — the single decision to make or artifact to build.
- **Non-goals** — what this session will explicitly not settle.
- **Format** — in-person, remote, or hybrid; duration available.
- **Constraints** — missing authority, research, or capacity that bounds
  what can be promised.

Do not promise outcomes that require a missing decider, missing research, or
missing implementation capacity.

### 2. Staff the room

- **Size:** 5–8 people optimal, 3–15 workable. Larger groups split into
  breakouts, never into audiences.
- **Decider:** one person with authority over the outcome. Mandatory for
  every decision block; ties break by supervote, not consensus.
- **Facilitator:** neutral owner of time, energy, and process. Contributes
  no content opinions.
- **Scribe:** captures outputs and the decision log in real time.
- **Doers and experts:** the people who will build on the outcome, plus
  domain experts for input blocks only.
- No spectators. No laptops or phones except the scribe's and the
  prototyping station's.

### 3. Assign pre-work

Pre-work covers whatever the session depends on: research, stakeholder
context, analytics, design files, technical constraints, decider input. Every
pre-work item names an owner and a deadline. If critical pre-work is missing
on the day, reschedule — do not improvise the foundation live.

### 4. Build the agenda

- **Diverge, then converge, per block.** Generate options silently and
  individually first (notes, sketches, HMWs); evaluate and rank together
  after. Silent generation beats brainstorming out loud.
- **Every block has a purpose and a tangible output.** Prefer making,
  mapping, ranking, deciding, and assigning ownership over discussion-only
  blocks. A block that produces only talk gets cut or converted.
- **Timebox everything and enforce it.** State the box up front, warn at
  halfway, close on time. An overrunning block steals from a later decision.
- **Manage energy.** Hard thinking in the morning; breaks at least every
  90 minutes; alternate solo, pair, and group modes.
- **Surface disagreement early.** Treat it as signal. Design exercises that
  force differences onto the wall (heat-map voting, speed critique) before
  converging, never after.
- **Park off-topic items visibly.** A parking lot the room can see keeps
  focus without losing ideas.
- **Remote sessions:** digital whiteboard prepared before the call, cameras
  on for decision blocks, shorter blocks than in-person, explicit check-ins
  at each transition.

### 5. Run the close

End with the decision log (decision, owner, date), deliverables with owners,
and a dated follow-up. Name unresolved decisions and risks clearly — an
unresolved item written down beats a false consensus every time.

## Output structure

Default to this order:

1. Workshop recommendation (format and why it fits)
2. Goals and non-goals
3. Participants and roles
4. Pre-work (item, owner, deadline)
5. Agenda with timeboxes (block, duration, purpose, output, materials)
6. Facilitation guide (per-block notes: diverge/converge mode, decision rule, traps)
7. Deliverables with owners
8. Follow-up plan plus named unresolved decisions and risks

For client-facing copy, keep it polished and concise and follow the voice in
`references/canon-brand-copy.md`. For internal facilitation, make it practical
and detailed enough that a different facilitator could run the session cold.

## Quality bar

- [ ] The session ends in a decision or an artifact — verified, not assumed
- [ ] Every agenda block states its purpose and its tangible output
- [ ] The Decider is present for every decision block
- [ ] Diverge precedes converge in every generative block
- [ ] Disagreement is surfaced by exercise design, early, not by debate late
- [ ] Pre-work items have owners and deadlines; gaps force reschedule
- [ ] No outcome is promised on missing authority, research, or capacity
- [ ] Unresolved decisions and risks are written down explicitly
