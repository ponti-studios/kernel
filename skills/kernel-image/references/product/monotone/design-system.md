# MØNØTØNÉ — Brand Design System

The brand bible for the MØNØTØNÉ product line. Read this before designing
or generating imagery for *any* MØNØTØNÉ product, regardless of category —
books, objects, tech accessories, or leather goods. Category files
(`books.md`, `objects.md`, `tech.md`, `leather.md`) apply these rules to a
specific product type; they never restate or override them.

## Brand Thesis

MØNØTØNÉ channels three references at once: **Apple** (restraint, obsessive
finish quality, typographic confidence), **Braun** (Dieter Rams-ian
functional minimalism, tactile material honesty, "as little design as
possible"), and **Hermès** (hand-craft signals, quiet material luxury, a
logo that whispers instead of shouts). Every MØNØTØNÉ product is a
considered object first and a branded object second. If a product idea
needs a loud logo, a busy pattern, or a gimmick to read as "on-brand," it
isn't — the object's own material, proportion, and one restrained motif
should carry the brand.

## Naming Convention

Every MØNØTØNÉ product belongs to a **neighborhood** — the line's
collection/SKU naming logic (e.g. SILVER LAKE, WILLIAMSBURG, SHOREDITCH).
A neighborhood is not just a name: it fixes one color, one material, and
one motif (see below) that every product in that neighborhood shares,
regardless of category. A "SILVER LAKE" book, tray, and phone stand all
carry the same sage-green cloth or leather, the same mid-century-house
line motif, and the same typographic treatment — they read as siblings,
not as separately branded items that happen to share a logo.

New neighborhoods should be named for a specific place with a distinct
character (a city neighborhood, a district, a small town) — never generic
category words ("Classic," "Premium") that don't anchor a color/motif
decision.

## Palette System

One fixed color, expressed in one fixed material, per neighborhood.
Established examples:

| Neighborhood | Color | Material |
|---|---|---|
| Silver Lake | Dark sage green | Premium woven cloth |
| Williamsburg | Matte charcoal black | Textured cloth |
| Shoreditch | Muted terracotta / burnt orange | Textured cloth |

Rule for new neighborhoods: pick one restrained, muted, single-material
color — never a bright/saturated hue, never more than one accent color on
a single object. If a product needs a second color to read correctly
(e.g. a metal fitting), that second color is always a neutral (brushed
steel, brass, black) — never a second brand hue.

## Typography & Marking

- Wordmark treatment is always **debossed or engraved into the material
  itself**, never printed, foiled in a contrasting color, or applied as a
  separate label — the mark should be felt, not just seen.
- Primary typography (product/neighborhood name) is large, clean,
  minimalist sans-serif, positioned with generous negative space.
- The collection wordmark "MØNØTØNÉ" (plus category suffix, e.g.
  "DISTRICTS" for books) appears small and discreet — never larger than
  necessary to be legible, always secondary to the neighborhood name.
- The **brushed-metal engraved brand plaque** — a small rectangular plaque
  reading "MØNØTØNÉ," engraved not printed — is the line's signature
  physical marker. It appears in-frame near the product in lifestyle and
  hero shots across every category, the way a hallmark stamp signals
  Hermès craftsmanship. It is not applied to the product itself, only
  staged alongside it.

## Motif Language

Every product carries exactly **one** subtle, fine-line, debossed or
etched motif tied to its neighborhood — a minimalist architectural or
place-specific line drawing (a mid-century house, an industrial grid
pattern, a brick pattern), never a busy or repeating all-over pattern.
The motif is decoration in the Rams sense: it exists because it earns its
place, not because empty space needs filling. If a product's silhouette
already communicates the neighborhood clearly, the motif can be smaller
or more restrained — never omitted entirely, since it's what makes a
product legibly MØNØTØNÉ rather than generically minimalist.

## Material Honesty Rules

**On-brand:** woven cloth, brushed or matte metal, concrete, unlacquered
or lightly-oiled wood, full-grain leather, raw materials where the grain,
weave, or texture is visible and honest about what the material is.

**Off-brand:** glossy plastic, chrome (mirror-polish reads as cheap, not
premium — brushed only), printed graphics standing in for a physical
material, busy or loud branding, faux textures (printed wood-grain,
printed leather-grain), any material that hides or fakes what it actually
is.

This is the section most likely to drift when a new product is
improvised — check any new product idea or generated image against this
list before treating it as on-brand.

## Photography Direction

The reusable studio system for all MØNØTØNÉ photography, across every
category — see `references/environment/lighting.md`'s Soft Diffused Studio
section for the base lighting reference this builds on:

- Soft, diffused studio lighting with gentle, realistic shadows that
  accentuate material texture (cloth grain, leather grain, metal brushing)
  and the depth of any debossing/engraving.
- Staging surface: minimalist, light gray textured concrete plinth against
  a clean, solid off-white studio background — the default staging for
  hero and detail shots across categories, unless a category file
  specifies an alternate surface appropriate to that product type.
- Hero angle: crisp three-quarter overhead view as the default, adaptable
  per category to whatever angle best shows the product's defining
  feature and any spine/edge/profile detail.
- Detail-grid convention: a lower-third grid of 2-3 close-up shots calling
  out specific material/craft details (debossing depth, stitching, edge
  finish, corner treatment) — reinforces the craft-quality claim that
  photography alone can't fully carry.
- High-end, sophisticated aesthetic: perfectly sharp focus, ultra-clean
  web-ready assets, 8K resolution.
- **Human models:** if a shot includes a human model (e.g. a lifestyle
  shot), the model renders per the skill's Universal Human Rendering Rule
  (`references/anime/style-guide.md`) — the product itself always stays
  photographic/realistic. This is the one deliberate stylistic departure
  from otherwise-photoreal studio photography.

## Brainstorm Prompts

Before writing a full product brief for a new MØNØTØNÉ product idea, run
it through these questions:

1. **What's the one motif?** Can you name a single fine-line, debossed
   place-motif for this product, tied to an existing or new neighborhood?
   If the idea needs multiple motifs or a busy pattern to feel complete,
   reconsider the concept.
2. **What's the single material?** Does the product have one honest,
   on-brand material driving its color and texture — not a composite of
   several unrelated materials?
3. **Which neighborhood?** Does this fit an existing neighborhood's
   established color/material, or does it warrant a new one? (Only
   introduce a new neighborhood if the product can't honestly fit an
   existing one.)
4. **Does it need the brass plaque?** Would the product be staged with the
   engraved brand plaque in-frame, or does its own engraving/debossing
   already carry the brand mark sufficiently?
5. **Would Rams keep it?** Strip the idea down to only what's functionally
   necessary — if a feature or flourish doesn't serve the object's
   function or the brand's one motif, cut it.
